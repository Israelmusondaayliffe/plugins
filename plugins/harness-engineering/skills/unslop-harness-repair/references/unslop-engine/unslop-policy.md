# Always-on Unslop policy

Apply this final pass to assistant-authored human-facing prose. The pass is mandatory even when the main task is not a writing task. Keep it proportional. A short direct answer needs a quick check, not the full editing pipeline.

## Protected exact material

Do not rewrite:

- code, shell commands, configuration, schemas, logs, or structured data
- direct quotations, citations, legal wording, or source evidence
- exact prompt blocks, model parameters, identifiers, paths, or user-required phrases

Apply Unslop only to the surrounding prose. In DETECT mode, leave supplied source text unchanged and clean the report itself.

## Quick pass

1. Cut chatbot openings, filler, puffery, vague attribution, promotional language, generic conclusions, and staged reveals.
2. Replace complex words with plain words when meaning stays intact.
3. Name the actor, mechanism, source, number, or next action when the draft supports one.
4. Break formulaic structures, forced triplets, false ranges, synonym cycling, fake contrasts, recap endings, and pull-quote lines.
5. Check rhythm. Preserve clear irregularity instead of polishing every sentence into the same shape.
6. Restore source-backed voice where editing flattened it.
7. Confirm that protected exact material and factual claims did not change.

## Source-backed human voice

Removing patterns is not enough. Sterile writing that erases the writer is also a failure.

- Preserve or sharpen opinions the writer supplied. Do not create a position for them.
- Preserve or clarify emotions the writer supplied. Do not invent a reaction.
- Keep first person when the source or explicit instruction supports it. Do not create experience or identity claims.
- Preserve mixed feelings, uncertainty, humor, bluntness, profanity, asides, detours, and rough edges when they carry meaning or character.
- Vary rhythm only when the source or intended voice supports it. Do not add random fragments to simulate humanity.
- Prefer concrete language. Specific facts, mechanisms, names, dates, and numbers beat generic claims of importance.

## Content and attribution

- Cut puffery and significance claims unless evidence explains the significance.
- Name media coverage, experts, studies, reports, critics, or sources. Otherwise qualify or remove the claim.
- Replace superficial trailing clauses with a real mechanism or delete them.
- Replace formulaic challenge-and-triumph framing with the specific constraint and result.
- Avoid promotional adjectives unless the format and evidence require them.

## Language and structure

- Prefer `is`, `has`, and direct verbs over theatrical substitutes.
- Remove binary contrast formulas when the second half can stand alone.
- Use the natural number of points. Do not force three.
- Repeat the clearest term instead of cycling synonyms.
- Use lists only when the material is genuinely parallel or sequential.
- Keep colons for lists, labels, quotations, and grammar. Remove staged colon reveals.
- Prefer active voice when the actor matters. Keep passive voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or deliberately backgrounded.
- Cut empty adverbs. Keep an adverb when it carries real emphasis, uncertainty, contrast, or voice.
- Split a sentence when a reader must backtrack. Keep a long sentence when its cadence is clear and characteristic.

## Specificity tests

- Mechanism test: ask what the sentence tells the reader to know or do. Add the supported mechanism or cut the sentence.
- Portability test: if a sentence could appear unchanged in another project's copy, it is probably too generic.
- Actor test: if an inanimate abstraction appears to decide, discover, believe, or act, name the actual person or process when known.
- Distance test: prefer the specific reader, team, or event over vague claims about people in general.
- Verb test: if an adverb props up a weak verb, use a more exact verb or the supported number.

Treat abstract metaphor nouns such as substrate, wedge, vector, locus, vantage, nexus, primitive, surface, bedrock, scaffolding, modality, gold-plating, ratchet, endgame, north star, and flywheel as context-sensitive warnings. Keep legitimate technical terms. Replace decorative abstraction with the concrete mechanism.

## Formatting

- Use sentence case for headings.
- Remove decorative emoji unless requested.
- Avoid bold-label-and-colon lists when prose reads better.
- Use straight quotation marks unless exact source typography must be preserved.
- Use no em dashes in authored prose under Israel's global rule.

## Final gate

Pass only when:

- the point is direct and specific
- supplied voice remains recognizable
- supplied opinions, emotions, and first-person stance remain clear
- no personality, experience, identity, facts, or examples were invented
- protected exact material is unchanged
- no obvious AI pattern remains without a documented reason
