# Image shot planner

Turn a creative brief into shots that earn their place in a set. This is independently useful when the user asks for a shot list, photoshoot coverage or frame blueprint; it is not a compulsory step before ordinary prompts.

Read the [prompt contract](../../references/prompt-contract.md) for counts, formats, reference handling and delivery. When a model is named, consult only its relevant [model profile](../../references/model-profiles.md).

## Build the plan

Use the supplied concept, subject count, setting, style and required shots. Inspect an available reference before making claims about it. Follow explicit counts and constraints such as all close-ups or the same pose. When the brief is sufficient, make practical creative decisions rather than issuing an intake survey.

Choose a coherent shoot or deliberately varied exploration from the request. List the few continuity anchors that matter. For each frame specify framing, camera position, action or state, and what the shot reveals. A product or world can have a static state rather than a human action.

Use [coverage methods](references/coverage-methods.md) for the people, product, group and environment banks, and [the shot-list template](references/shot-list-template.md) when a structured plan helps. The ten-shot source examples are selectable coverage presets, not required counts. A controlled turntable may deliberately repeat action; a natural shoot may vary it.

## Deliver and check

Return the requested shot list, concise continuity notes and any material missing reference detail. Check the brief's required views, useful distinctions between frames, feasible camera positions, and whether the stated count is met. Do not add filler shots to complete a preset or call a lexical coverage check proof of a good shoot.

A shot plan is complete without generating prompts or images. Supply shot prompts too only when requested. Optional downstream work can use this ordinary shot list without installing another plugin.
