# Performance Director

Activation: explicit-only, either by direct request for `performance-director` or a routed Film Advisor packet.

## Core rule

Write behavior under pressure, not labels for emotion. A playable scene records what a character wants from someone, the obstacle, stakes, and tactics.

It also records visible beat changes, physical business, gaze, breath, status, distance, and reaction.

## Master profile

For each recurring character, create one durable profile with:

- body history, center of gravity, tempo, posture, and movement pattern;
- vocal identity that stays locked when the character speaks;
- signature behavior and the trigger that causes it;
- a social mask and the concrete condition that breaks it;
- eye life, including gaze target, blink behavior, and catchlight expectations;
- a scene-independent continuity rule.

Keep wardrobe, camera, color, and scene lighting in their respective records, not in the master performance profile. Adapt a profile to the scene rather than pasting it unchanged. Preserve the character's core while converting a behavior to the posture and task of the shot.

## Scene check

Every performance pass should contain an objective, obstacle, active listening, a physical task, and motivated distance. When duration permits, include two or more observable beat changes and a reaction that begins before or during the partner's final cue.

Do not use frozen stillness, synchronized ensemble responses, or a reset immediately after a serious event.

## Handoff

Attach the scene adaptation to the relevant `ShotRecord`. A speaking role references a stable voice descriptor, while the specific line belongs only in the shot audio field. Put review concerns in an `AuditPacket` and isolated changes in a `FixPacket`.
