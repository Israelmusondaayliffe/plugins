# Proof To Product Mapper

Choose the form that fits the evidence, not the most impressive container.

## Shared rules

Use [source and tool policy](../../references/source-and-tool-policy.md) when
current alternatives, demand, pricing, or distribution affect the choice.
Follow
[evidence and artifact policy](../../references/evidence-and-artifact-policy.md)
when grading proof and making claims.

## Grade the evidence

Identify the underlying problem, beneficiary, repeated method, observed result,
and source of each claim. Classify the opportunity:

- Proven: repeated evidence supports the result in the relevant context.
- Promising: some evidence exists, but important uncertainty remains.
- Unproven: the work is primarily an idea or proposal.

Promising and unproven work may continue, but the output must emphasize
validation rather than productization certainty.

## Compare forms

Choose across three separate axes:

- Delivery form: guide, template, workshop, service, software tool, skill,
  plugin, or operating system.
- Access model: internal, private client, member or community, or public.
- Economics: free, cost-recovery, paid, sponsored, or undecided.

Compare only plausible delivery forms against:

- user need and context of use
- evidence strength and repeatability
- required support and maintenance
- distribution and discoverability
- customization versus standardization
- risk, rights, and privacy
- effort to reach a useful minimum version

Search current alternatives when market context could change the recommendation.
Do not invent demand, revenue, or differentiation.

## Map the next version

Recommend one primary delivery form, access model, economics choice, and a
strong alternative. Create the smallest useful scope, intended user, promise,
inputs, outputs, exclusions, evidence, risks, distribution hypothesis, and
acceptance test. For promising or unproven work, design the cheapest validation
that could change the choice.

Use [the Productization Brief template](assets/productization-brief-template.md).
Building, publishing, selling, or installing the resulting product requires a
separate request and authorization.
